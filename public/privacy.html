A little privacy.

SLOT is Walter's personal invitation tool. Sending a plan shares your name, proposed Berlin date and time, and note with Walter. You do not need an account or an email address.

The private backend stores the proposal and delivery status to prevent duplicate messages. Records remain there until Walter removes them; emailed copies remain in his Gmail. Ask Walter if you want your proposal removed. Invitation codes are stored as hashes. A short-lived, keyed hash of the connection IP limits abuse. SLOT adds no analytics or advertising trackers.

GitHub hosts the public page. Cloudflare hosts the backend and private owner login. These providers process ordinary connection and security data under their own policies. Google authorization is restricted to Walter's account and used to send proposal emails to himself. SLOT requests basic identity and Gmail send permission, with no mailbox reading or calendar access. The renewable Gmail credential is encrypted in the backend.

An invitation lets its holder send one proposal. It does not reserve a time or add anything to a calendar. Do not include sensitive information in the note.
